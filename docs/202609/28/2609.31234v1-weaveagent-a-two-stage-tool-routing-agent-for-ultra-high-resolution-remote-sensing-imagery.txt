Abstract Problem. Ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing with vague user intents poses two bottlenecks: visual tokens are expensive , and tool calling must be format-reliable (pretrained models emit zero tool calls zero-shot). Method. WeaveAgent, a two-stage tool-routing agent, decouples routing decisions from visual perception. Stage A is routing-first: emission is trained, not elicited. Stage B executes conditionally: intrinsic queries enter visual answering (full-scene thumbnail; the WeaveEarth-style evidence board as an optional fixed-budget, ≈5k-token com-pression interface with a reported 1.31 × detail ceiling and negative paired deltas); extrinsic queries execute T_call on the original full-resolution imagery, answering from tool observa-tions in a second, observation-masked round (two-turn trajectories). Training: alignment SFT, then GRPO under the routing-first reward RWA2 . Results. Alignment SFT lifts extrinsic routing from 0% to 80.75% (323/400); GRPO suppresses 9 intrinsic mis-emissions while tool selection is unchanged. The trained 2B system does not beat the zero-shot 8B baseline overall (0.263 vs. 0.250 ) — a diagnostic, not leaderboard, contribution. Oracle attribution separates the two ingredients of the repair: loading the observation into context already lifts extrinsic answer accuracy from 0.025 to 0.425 under marker-free cross-mode returns, and the two-turn SFT stage adds a further significant +9 .3 points to 0.518 (0.965 under training-distribution returns; all oracle/annotation-grounded) at a small routing cost. A ±-image ablation shows emission suppression is visually grounded, and a query-register matrix shows LLM-rewritten queries cost the trained checkpoints 2–11 points. Scope. All training and evaluation use the 5,000 / 3,273 / 1,000-record VagueUHR corpus (600 intrinsic + 400 tool-requiring; the 5,000-record base seeds the synthesis pipeline and is not itself used for optimization). Single-pass evi-dence construction runs at 7.31 s per image on an RTX 4090 — faster than reported multi-round search (cross-hardware reference). Code, data, and all evaluation protocols will be released. 

# 1 Introduction 

Ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing (RS) images — sub-meter scenes that routinely exceed 4,000–10,000 pixels per side — stress vision-language models (VLMs) in a specific way: resizing the scene to the model’s native input discards exactly the dense small objects that make UHR imagery informative, while pushing the original resolution through the visual encoder makes the token cost grow out of control (the images evaluated in this work measure 6,044 px per side at the median and 27,328 px at the maximum; Figure 1 shows both difficulties on one real record). The two bottlenecks point to two capabilities of a different nature. Perception is expensive : every visual token costs, and should be spent only after the decision “what needs to be seen” has been made. 

Decision is cheap : the information needed to judge “does this query need a tool, and which one” lives almost entirely in the query text and the tool catalog. This is not speculation but a literature-backed design basis: Toolformer [3] showed that a language model can learn when to call, what to call, and with which arguments from purely textual self-supervision, and RouteLLM [4] showed that a router looking only at the query text can halve cost without quality loss and generalizes across models. When these findings are transported to UHR remote sensing, the natural architecture is to decouple routing decisions from visual perception : a text-first decision stage decides before any visual token is spent on answering; visual tokens enter the context only afterwards, on the path the decision selects. Our own ±-image ablation then shows the strong form of this design basis is only partially attained: the trained router’s emission suppression is visually grounded (Section 5.6) — a boundary we measure and report rather than assume away. 

The WeaveAgent two-stage architecture. Stage A (Section 3.2) is a routing-first decision stage : its decision interface is built around the query q and the tool catalog C (a text-first decision interface), delegates intrinsic queries to answering, and for extrinsic queries selects a tool and emits 

T_call (ek, p ). Stage B (Section 3.3–Section 3.4) executes conditionally: the intrinsic path feeds the visual input (full-scene thumbnail, optionally the evidence board) to the model to generate the answer; the extrinsic path executes the T_call — tools always operate on the original full-resolution imagery — backfills the tool observation (boxes, masks, counts) into the context, and generates the final answer in a second round (two-turn trajectories with observation masking [25]). Decoupling yields three direct benefits: (i) the decision stage is a single cheap text-first step (Section 5.6 2quantifies how far a fully text-only router remains from the trained policy); (ii) the T_call output is a purely textual decision that alignment training can make reliably parseable; and (iii) the tool path suffers no visual-compression loss, because tools see the original image. 

Repositioning the evidence board. We adopt the WeaveEarth [1] structured evidence pipeline (GCC scoring, MSES compression, TPEB/SEM assembly) as an optional compression interface of the Stage-B intrinsic path, and recalibrate its position honestly: it is not an accuracy booster — on three static VQA benchmarks its paired deltas against the 2048-px thumbnail baseline are −1.0

(LRS-VQA), −3.4 (MME-RS) and −2.9 (XLRS) points, and its components show no positive con-tribution in ablations (Section 5.3); it is a fixed-budget compressed visual interface — any UHR image is compressed into two structured views of ≈5k tokens with constant token cost regardless of source size, trading a 1.31 × detail ceiling (Section 6.3) for determinism and local-structure preser-vation on fine-grained task types. We report all of its properties under this “compression interface, not booster” positioning. 

Training recipe: alignment SFT → RWA2 GRPO, a positive gain per component. Tool routing does not appear for free: the zero-shot model emits zero T_call calls, and GRPO from the base model cannot explore the behavior spontaneously (85–90% of rollout groups have zero reward variance). Our recipe has two steps. Alignment SFT seeds the emission behavior with prompts and image formats strictly identical to evaluation, lifting extrinsic routing from 0% to 80.75% (323/400, tool selection 0.905). GRPO with the routing-first reward RWA2 then stabilizes on top: suppressing 9 intrinsic mis-emissions (classification 0.435 → 0.460 , reasoning 0.435 → 0.450 ) while extrinsic tool selection is unchanged (323/400), with healthy training dynamics (zero-variance groups an order of magnitude below the unaligned arms, sequence lengths stable at 165–180 tokens). Under this recipe a third gap surfaced and was addressed: with routing at 80.75%, extrinsic answer accuracy stayed near zero — an observation →answer conversion gap . An oracle attribution study (Section 5.7) localizes the break to the conversion of marker-free tool text (argument values are ≈96% correct while final answers score 0.045 ), and extrinsic answer accuracy rises from 0.025 to 0.425 by loading the observation alone, and to 0.518 with the two-turn SFT stage (McNemar p = 3 .8 × 10 −5), under marker-free cross-mode returns (0.965 under training-distribution returns), at a small routing cost. Query wording is not a hidden confound: on 992 paired records the zero-shot floor is robust to LLM-rewritten natural phrasing ( +3 .1 points, McNemar p = 0 .043 ; Section 5.5), and the trained checkpoints degrade gracefully but measurably on the rewritten register (Section 5.5).

# 2 Related Work 

UHR remote sensing understanding: passive vs. active perception. Passive methods push more pixels or tokens through the model: GeoLLaVA-8K [19] extends the visual context to 8K, and token-pruning pipelines compress gigapixel inputs at inference time [6]. Active methods let the model zoom and search: ZoomEye [20], ZoomEarth [21] and ZoomSearch [22] perform multi-round visual exploration driven by the language model, at multi-round latency cost (Section 5.4). WeaveEarth [1] reframes the problem as evidence organization : candidate patches scored by a cross-modal encoder (SigLIP2 [10]), greedily compressed into a minimal support set, and arranged on a topology-preserving board with structured metadata; it reports 33.38/47.38/47.14% on LRS-VQA / MME-RealWorld-RS / XLRS-Bench with a frozen Qwen3-VL-8B [8]. All of these are tool-free :whatever they cannot resolve from pixels and prompts, they cannot answer. WeaveAgent adopts the evidence pipeline as Stage-B’s optional compression interface (Section 3.3) and adds full tool-routing capability on top. 

RS vision-language models and instruction tuning. A parallel line trains RS-specialized MLLMs by instruction tuning: GeoChat [31] grounds conversations in RS imagery, EarthGPT [32] 4and its spatial successor EarthGPT-X [33] unify multisensor, multitask interpretation under one MLLM, RSGPT [34] and LHRS-Bot-Nova [35] pair large instruction corpora with dedicated bench-marks, RS-LLaVA [36] couples captioning with VQA, VHM [38] adds explicit anti-hallucination training, SkySenseGPT [37] builds a 1.8M-sample instruction corpus on a multiscale RS foundation model, and RS5M/GeoRSCLIP [39] scale vision-language pretraining for RS retrieval. These mod-els broaden what can be answered from pixels, but answer every query through the same monolithic generate-from-pixels interface: none decides whether external capabilities are needed, nor routes to tool backends — the axis our two-stage router addresses. WeaveAgent is complementary rather than competing: its Stage-B intrinsic path is exactly such a VLM, and the trained router sits in front of it. 

Agentic remote sensing and RL for tool use. RS-Agent [23], EarthAgent [24] and Open-EarthAgent [25] orchestrate expert models for earth observation. Two OpenEarthAgent designs are adopted directly by our Stage-B extrinsic path: two-turn trajectories with observation masking 

(tool-observation tokens are excluded from the training loss, so the policy learns to use observations rather than recite them) and schema-registration-as-integration (tools enter the catalog as declar-ative schemas, no code changes). RemoteAgent [2] is closest to ours: it defines the vague-intent routing problem, the T_call action space and the VagueEO protocol (10 task shapes, 5 unseen in training), reporting 95.0% intent-recognition accuracy with Qwen2.5-VL-7B [9], but its router consumes a downscaled image and its GRPO stage covers intrinsic shapes only, leaving tool routing a zero-shot capability. Vague queries as such are not unique to remote sensing: ambiguous and underspecified visual questions have a longer history in VQA, both as datasets that ground what an ambiguous question could refer to [26] and as models that learn to ask clarifying follow-ups [27]; RemoteAgent is the work that carried this axis into EO agents, and VagueUHR extends it to UHR imagery with tool trajectories. Our VagueUHR corpus, explicit tool-selection reward, and two-stage training recipe are direct responses; we additionally provide both a TRL-based path [15] and an ms-swift configuration mirroring the paper’s stack (Section 3.5). GRPO [11] group-normalizes sequence-level rewards and underpins verifiable-reward (RLVR) training [12]; RemoteAgent applies answer-verifiable rewards (Hungarian-matched IoU, relative-error decay, label coverage). Also di-rectly relevant to our cold-start strategy are ToRL’s tool-integrated RL (forced tool calls to seed exploration) [28], ToolRL’s decomposition of tool rewards into format and selection terms [29], and DAPO’s treatment of zero-variance groups [30] — together supporting “SFT seeds the behavior, RL amplifies it” pipelines (Sections 3.5, 6.1 and 6.2). 

Text-only routing and cascades. Stage-A’s design builds on a line of text-only routing work. Toolformer [3] samples call sites from plain text corpora under self-supervision, letting a model learn when to call, which API, and with which arguments — evidence that tool-calling decisions are essentially text-decidable. RouteLLM [4] trains routers that see only the query text to dispatch between strong and weak models, roughly halving cost without quality loss on several benchmarks and generalizing across models — evidence that query text carries sufficient routing signal. Earlier cascading and query-complexity routing practice is consistent with both. WeaveAgent transfers this conclusion to extrinsic/intrinsic discrimination and tool selection in UHR remote sensing with a text-first decision interface (Section 3.2); our own ±-image ablation then shows the transfer is only partial — the trained policy’s emission suppression is visually grounded (Section 5.6). 

Benchmarks and tool backends. LRS-VQA [6], the remote-sensing subset of MME-RealWorld [7] and XLRS-Bench [5] are the standard UHR RS evaluation suites (Section 5.1 details our stratified-5sampling protocol). For dense prediction we rely on segmentation backends in the Segment Anything family [17, 18], exposed through MCP. 

Differences from the closest systems. Relative to WeaveEarth [1]: we add two-stage tool routing and training; its evidence pipeline is adopted as Stage-B’s optional compression interface, with the recalibrated positioning of Section 3.3. Relative to RemoteAgent [2]: we add an explicit training signal for the routing decision stage (alignment SFT + RWA2 ), a two-turn tool-execution path with an oracle attribution and repair of the conversion gap, a runnable training stack, and a public corpus. Relative to zoom-and-search methods [20, 22]: single-pass text-first routing plus single-pass evidence construction replaces multi-round visual exploration (efficiency comparison in Section 5.4). 

# 3 The WeaveAgent Two-Stage Architecture 

WeaveAgent is implemented as the weaveagent package with six subsystems — evidence , skills ,

agents , mcp , training , data_synthesis — twelve built-in tools behind a declarative MCP registry with automatic discovery, and a publish/subscribe multi-agent layer. The skills subsystem wraps every callable capability behind a uniform interface (five built-in skill families — evidence retrieval, detection, segmentation, counting, temporal comparison — front the twelve built-in tools, and any MCP-registered tool can be exposed as a skill without code changes); the agents subsystem provides typed-event orchestration, and for all evaluations below the router runs as one agent over this layer, keeping framework overhead out of the reported latencies.

3.2 Stage A: Text-Only Router 

Input and output. Stage A reads two pieces of text: the query q and the tool catalog C, the latter rendered automatically from the declarative MCP registry [16] (tool names, functional descriptions, argument schemas). Its output is the routing decision of Eq. (1): intrinsic queries are delegated to answering; extrinsic queries select a tool ek and emit T_call (ek, p ), with p grounded in the catalog’s region syntax. Final answering after the tool observation returns belongs to Stage B (Section 3.4). 

Design basis. Tool-routing decisions are largely determined by the query text: Toolformer [3] shows that pure-text self-supervision suffices to learn the three elements of tool calling (when, what, with which arguments); RouteLLM [4] shows that text-only routing halves cost without quality loss and generalizes across models. Stage-A’s decision interface is therefore designed to need no visual tokens: this would decouple routing cost from image size entirely (routing a 27,328-px image costs the same as routing a 4,096-px one), and would reduce “routing generalization to unseen tools” to a pure-text problem — adding one schema entry to the catalog; the sm3det measurement of Section 4.3 shows registration alone does not deliver this ( 0/62 on the unseen tool), so this remains design intent, not a demonstrated property. In the unified prompt of Section 3.1 the visual inputs are nonetheless present. Our own ±-image ablation (E19; Section 5.6) shows that the strong form of this design basis does not hold for the trained 2B policy: with image tokens removed (identical prompts and records), extrinsic routing is preserved ( 0.8375 vs. 0.8075 ) but intrinsic routing collapses ( 0.985 → 0.0217 )and the model emits a call on almost every record (987 of 1,000). The visual input is the signal that suppresses emission on intrinsic queries: knowing when not to call is visually grounded in UHR imagery. The 15 × latency gap ( 0.257 s vs. 3.85 s per record) makes a truly text-only router an attractive but unattained target; distilling one is future work (Section 7). 7Action space. Following RemoteAgent, extrinsic decisions emit the hybrid action T_call (ek, p )

with ek chosen from the injected catalog. The behavior is strictly binary — emit a T_call or delegate to answering — with no degenerate output forms (smoke audit, E12). The direct ablation of zero-visual-token vs. image-conditioned routing has been run (E19, Section 5.6): removing image tokens keeps extrinsic routing but collapses intrinsic routing and re-opens emission on almost every record, so Stage A rests on the literature above, on the end-to-end routing measurements of Section 5.2, and on this measured boundary of the text-only design basis.

Positioning: compression interface, not booster. The value proposition is token determin-ism , not accuracy: token cost is fixed (two views, ≈5k tokens) regardless of source size, whereas the naive detail-preserving alternative (high-resolution crop stitching) has a token cost that grows linearly with source size and detail demand, and the thumbnail baseline, though equally fixed-cost, abandons local detail entirely. 3.4 Stage B (Extrinsic Path): Tool Execution, Observation Backfill, and Two-Turn Trajectories 

Execution. After Stage A emits T_call (ek, p ), the system executes the tool on the original full-resolution image — the extrinsic path passes through no visual compression, so detection, segmen-tation and counting precision are bounded only by the tool backend itself. Backends are pluggable with a three-tier fallback (native MCP stdio servers → released HTTP expert services → generic JSON endpoints), so the same router drives both real deployments (e.g., DirectSAM-class segmen-tation [18]) and annotation-grounded simulated backends; we disclose per experiment which tools are real. 

Observation backfill and two-turn trajectories. Tool outputs (detection boxes, mask statis-tics, counts) are serialized into a structured observation o and backfilled into the context; the second round generates the final <answer> from (q, C, o ). Training follows OpenEarthAgent’s [25] two-turn trajectories with observation masking : observation tokens are excluded from the loss, so the policy learns to use observations rather than recite them. 

Schema registration as integration. Because tools register by declarative schema and the catalog is text (Section 3.2), an unseen tool can be injected into the catalog after training to test routing generalization without retraining (Section 4.3). 

Known gap, diagnosed and partially repaired. With extrinsic routing at 80.75%, final-answer accuracy on extrinsic tasks remained low (detection 0.035 –0.045 , segmentation 0.005 across E13/E15 under the single-turn protocol) — an observation →answer conversion gap . An oracle attribution study (Section 5.7) localizes the break to the conversion of marker-free tool text rather than to calling quality, and a two-turn trajectory SFT stage (Section 3.6, evaluated in Section 5.7) partially repairs it under protocol caveats disclosed there. 

3.5 Training Recipe: Alignment SFT → RWA2 GRPO

# 4 The VagueUHR Corpus 

No public corpus links vague UHR intents to tool-call trajectories: VagueEO [2] ships an evaluation protocol but trains on intrinsic tasks only, and WeaveEarth-style benchmarks contain answers but no tool traces. VagueUHR fills the gap synthetically : trajectories are constructed on top of established UHR benchmarks so that every answer is derived from existing annotations and never model-generated. This section is kept deliberately brief; every disclosure in it is substantive. 

4.1 Synthesis Pipeline 

Records are bootstrapped from LRS-VQA, MME-RealWorld-RS and XLRS-Bench imagery and annotations. The pipeline has three stages: (i) a query generator maps each source record to one of ten task shapes and emits a deliberately vague query via type-preserving vagueness operators from a template bank; (ii) a trajectory synthesizer constructs the reference plan — an intrinsic answer, or T_call (tool , region ) steps grounded in the annotations — together with the assumed tool catalog; (iii) a rule-based validator checks query–annotation–trajectory consistency, schema compliance, and jargon leakage, rejecting any failing record; the shipped corpus passes validation at 100%. 4.4 Honest Labeling 

Four disclosures: (i) all template-split records carry vague_level = 0 .5 and source = heuristic — vagueness is templated and narrower than open-ended user phrasing; (ii) 11% of test answers are unseen in training ; (iii) 34 loose-count queries carry hedge wording; (iv) the six test records drawn from the two 1213 source images (the cluster flagged by the annotation audit) were manually re-checked against the imagery: five labels were confirmed, and one was identified as a source-annotation misread (the “surrounding area” query labeled urban whose surroundings are rural); the record is flagged for correction in the next corpus revision and is retained here unchanged so that the test set remains byte-identical to the released version. The 8 removals in the rewritten split are precisely the output of the annotation–image consistency check. 

# 5 Experiments 

5.1 Setup and Stratified-Sampling Protocol 

Benchmarks and stratified sampling. We evaluate on LRS-VQA (7,333 questions; 8 cat-egories), the remote-sensing subset of MME-RealWorld (3,738 multiple-choice questions; Color, Count, Position), and XLRS-Bench-lite (3,080). Because full-benchmark sweeps are prohibitive on our single-GPU budget, all our numbers are computed on category-proportional stratified samples: 1,500 records per benchmark for the compression-interface characterization (seed 0; all 8 LRS cat-egories covered with proportions matching the full set; MME position 33.6% / color 33.5% / count 32.9%), 500 per variant for the component ablations and the k-scan, and 300 for the official-script comparison. Stratified estimates are unbiased but carry sampling noise; we therefore report paired 

evidence-on/off comparisons on identical samples and, where possible, McNemar tests. Our strat-ified estimates are not directly comparable to paper-reported full-benchmark numbers , and we never place them in the same row without saying so. On XLRS we can run 7 of the 8 subcategories (SR is unavailable to us; OP contributes 53.9% of our sample), so per-subcategory columns cannot be matched to the WeaveEarth paper’s Table 2; overall (weighted) accuracy remains comparable. 14 Backbone-to-claim map. Two backbones appear, each tied to specific claims: the trained 2B (Qwen3-VL-2B, all of E13/E15/E17) carries every routing, reward, and conversion result (Sec-tions 5.2 and 5.7); the zero-shot 8B (Qwen3-VL-8B, E3a and the E14 wording arms) is used only as the zero-shot floor and its wording-robustness check, plus the compression- and efficiency-interface characterizations (Sections 5.3 and 5.4), which involve no training. No table mixes backbones within a comparison; the 2B scale limitation is Section 7 item 1. 

Decoding and matching. All evaluations use greedy decoding (temperature 0), matching the official scripts: paired comparisons are the measurements most sensitive to decoding noise, and a 

±1–2-point sampling-noise floor swamps most claimed gains at these sample sizes — our earlier sampling-mode runs were an accident; those outputs are quarantined — the quarantine boundary is every sampling-mode output directory, and none of them enters any table — and no number in this paper comes from them. Answer matching follows WeaveEarth’s released rules (normalized exact / integer / substring / token-F1 ≥ 0.8) plus MCQ letter matching; MCQ option instructions must be ordered after the SEM region metadata (Section 6.4). 

Backbones and hardware. Compression-interface and intrinsic-path evaluations use Qwen3-VL-8B (matching WeaveEarth) served by vLLM 0.27.1 [14] (FP8) on a single RTX 4090 (24 GB); routing training and all two-stage evaluations use Qwen3-VL-2B. RemoteAgent’s official numbers use Qwen2.5-VL-7B under its own protocol and are reported separately; we do not co-scale them with ours. 

Positioning with respect to public benchmarks. All headline numbers are measured on VagueUHR-test rather than on public RS suites. This is deliberate: the quantities under study — whether to call, what to call, and whether a call converts into an executed observation — are properties of the query–tool interaction protocol, not of single-image perception, and public suites expose no executable tool backends, so none of our headline metrics is reproducible on them. Cross-model perception rankings on those suites [31, 32, 34, 35] answer a different question and are treated as related work, not baselines; the tool-execution ceiling is instead bounded from above by the oracle arms (Section 5.7).

6.1 Why Alignment SFT Is Necessary: Two Mechanisms 

Mechanism 1: the exploration deadlock — GRPO cannot create behavior from noth-ing. GRPO is group-relative: advantage A = ( r − ¯r)/(std + ϵ). When the policy never emits a tool call, all four rollouts in a group are direct answers, rewards are identical, std = 0 , all advan-tages are 0, and the gradient is zero. Measured GRPO from the 2B base: initial reward ≈0.058 ,

frac_reward_zero_std = 0 .85 –0.9 — 85–90% of groups produce no learning signal. The zero-shot zero-emission floor (top rows of Table 3) is this mechanism’s direct projection: GRPO can only amplify variance in behavior that already exists. Competing hypotheses were excluded one by one: a loose regex re-scan of all raw evaluation outputs finds 0/4,000 containing T_call (not a parser false negative); the median tool-call completion is p50 = 193 characters ≈70 –80 tokens, below max_new_tokens = 128 (not truncation); the evaluation prompt is confirmed to contain the 15-line tool catalog (not a missing catalog). Mechanism 2: format binding — emission is bound to the prompt distribution. This is the entire meaning of “alignment” in the recipe’s first step. 

6.2 Why the Incentive Must Be Routing-First: Reward Economics 

RWA ’s structure makes direct answering expected-optimal. From Table 17 ( wt = 0 .3,no-call consolation 0.2): a correct call on an extrinsic task caps at 0.35 , a correct direct answer scores 1.11 , and calling on intrinsic tasks (73% of training data, 2,400/3,273) scores 0; aggregating (Table 1), always answering directly ( 0.11+ pans ) beats correct routing ( 0.35 ) once pans ≥ 0.24 — the RL-rational policy is “treat tool tasks as answer tasks”. Measurements agree: under E12-RWA mean completion length converges to 16–25 tokens (i.e. no calls), and under RRA RL actively removes 

emission (a bare T_call has no <answer> span and scores a constant zero; mean length collapses to 8.5–9.7 tokens) — even SFT-seeded behavior is washed out by a wrongly structured reward. 

RWA2 inverts the expectation. The three structural modifications (delete the no-call consolation score ( 0.2 → 0), raise the routing weight ( 0.3 → 1.0), and split the format term into two 0.5

components — correct direct answers actually rise ( 1.11 → 1.25 ), but correct routed calls rise more (0.35 → 2.25 )) invert the expectations: correct routing 2.25 ≫ direct answering 0.25 –1.25 per case (a correct route is credited with the answer term automatically), and the inversion is parameter-free — it holds for any answer accuracy (Table 1). E15 measurements (Table 5): initial reward 1.18 , zero-variance groups an order of magnitude below the unaligned arms, mean length stable at 165–180 

— emission locked in by the reward structure; on the evaluation side, tool selection 0.905 → 0.914 

(+0 .9 points on overall routing, i.e., the 9 suppressed intrinsic mis-emissions; extrinsic tool selection unchanged at 323/400) and overall accuracy 0.2430 → 0.2500 (Section 5.2). 

Design conclusion. RWA2 ’s value shape is “maintain and suppress” rather than “raise further”: under the single-turn evaluation protocol, most of the tool-selection gain is already claimed by align-ment SFT (80.75%); RWA2 stabilizes emission on the evaluation distribution, eliminates intrinsic mis-emissions, and squeezes out a further +0 .9 points of tool selection. Without alignment SFT the reward-design difference is invisible (the two E12 arms tie at evaluation) — alignment and incentive must be repaired together, which is why the recipe’s two steps are inseparable. 28 6.3 The Boundary of the Compression Interface: the 1.31 × Ceiling and Condi-tions of Applicability 

The ceiling. The TPEB gives each evidence cell a fixed 448-px patch on a 6×6 grid; relative to the 2048-px full-scene thumbnail, the effective detail gain of a selected region is 448 × 6/2048 ≈ 1.31 ×

— constant regardless of the source image’s resolution : a 27,328-px scene earns the same 31% as a 4,096-px one. The board also covers only 6 of 36 cells (a sixth of the field of view) and destroys global spatial continuity. Two mechanisms explain the full negative shape of Tables 6 and 7: (i) tasks that depend on whole-scene layout do not benefit — MME-RS position questions lose −10 .9

points paired, because local detail cannot compensate for lost global geometry; (ii) retrieval is recall-bounded — if the GCC scorer (Eq. (2)) does not rank the relevant region into the top-8 anchors, the downstream VLM never sees it, and the budget-k selection (Eq. (3)) only compounds this by design. This mechanism is now a measurement, not an assertion: a retrieval audit over the 660 spatial-ground-truth test records (gt-box-center criterion) measures recall@8 anchors = 0 .291 and recall@6 final board = 0 .230 — 71% of queried targets are never reachable through the evidence board, and MSES compression discards a further 6 points; per task type the picture is uniform (0.20–0.32 across types, per-type detail in Section C; counting, n=2 , has no spatial ground truth and is excluded). Query–patch similarity retrieval is weakest exactly where UHR tasks are hardest (whole-scene counting, spatial relations, change detection). 

Interventional repairs (harder boundary evidence than observation). After the three-benchmark paired negatives, we systematically tried four families of repairs, five configurations in total (paired, greedy, seed-0 stratified 300 records, baseline = interface off): routing spatial questions to the global view (MME +1 .35 , p = 0 .65 ; LRS −2.1, p = 0 .41 , with reasoning −9.3 from “where is” misrouting); removing the thumbnail for detail questions with k=4 large cells (MME 

−4.7, p = 0 .08 , color −10 .1); keeping the thumbnail with k=4 native-resolution cells (MME +0 .34 

/ LRS −1.7; shape +2 .8 but count −10 .3); and a four-quadrant coverage guarantee (neutral). None significantly beats the thumbnail baseline (all p ∈ [0 .08 , 1.0] ), and directions contradict each other. 

The mechanism explanation is consistent: the board’s detail advantage is locked at ≈1.31 × by the 448-px budget; raising per-cell resolution necessarily reduces cell count, and the coverage loss cancels the gain; “spatial questions to the global view” is the only stably positive component ( ≈2

points). 

Design conclusion. No positive component contribution on static VQA (Table 7), a flat k

curve (Table 8), and no significant repair — together these support the positioning of Section 3.3: 

TPEB +SEM is a fixed-budget compression interface whose benefit lies in token determinism and local-structure preservation for fine-grained task types, not in average accuracy . Enable it per task type (fine-grained grounding), not by default. 

6.4 Implementation Differences and Prompt Sensitivity (Neutral Records) 

This subsection records differences between the WeaveEarth released implementation and the pa-per’s algorithm, and prompt-format sensitivity, as neutral protocol-alignment records; Table 18 (Section B) collects the measurements, and Figure 12 (Section C) plots the Category-line control. (i) Under the paired greedy protocol the released script’s own gain is +2 .67 points (29.67% →

32.33%; 89 vs. 97 of 300 correct, 24 vs. 32 discordant pairs, McNemar exact p = 0 .350 ) versus the paper-reported +6 .70 (26.68% → 33.38%) on the full set; the paper’s baseline configuration (prompt, decoding) is not documented, so we record the protocol gap and treat the paper and the 29 release as two distinct objects. (ii) The released MSES selection truncates the candidate pool to the top-8 anchors before the budget greedy, so the greedy loop never executes; we reimplement per the paper’s algorithm (Section 3.3) and audited all shared components item by item. (iii) A Category task-type line present only in the treatment arm had been inferred, by cross-protocol inference, to contribute ≈3.7 points; the paired control on the same 300 samples (60 per task type, E15 check-point, greedy) gives 0.2667 vs. 0.2833 — ∆ = −1.7 points with the line, McNemar exact p = 0 .51 ,per-type movement a reshuffle — so the Category line does not leak answer information and the 

≈3.7-point inference is refuted. (iv) The mere order of MCQ option instructions versus the SEM metadata block swings accuracy by ≈10 .5 points, silently (quarantined self-audit, indicative; 34% of outputs degrade to prose that fails every matching rule); we therefore adopt two rules through-out: byte-identical prompt templates across baseline and treatment arms except for the mechanism under test, and McNemar tests on paired binary outcomes rather than point deltas alone. 

# 7 Limitations 

1. Backbone scale. 8B GRPO does not fit a 24-GB GPU, so all two-stage training and evaluation use Qwen3-VL-2B (the zero-shot floor row of Table 3 is 8B). Scaling the alignment pipeline to 8B (alignment SFT → RWA2 GRPO re-run at 8B, requiring ≥48 GB) and re-measuring the routing table at that scale remains future work. 2. Emission suppression is visually grounded; a truly text-only router remains unattained. 

The ±-image ablation (E19; Section 5.6) shows that removing image tokens keeps extrinsic rout-ing ( 0.8375 vs. 0.8075 ) but collapses intrinsic routing ( 0.985 → 0.022 ) and re-opens emission on almost every record (987/1,000) — at 15 × lower latency ( 0.257 s vs. 3.85 s per record). Knowing 

when not to call is visually grounded in UHR imagery; distilling a genuinely text-only router is future work. 3. Wording robustness is now measured for the aligned checkpoints, but register cov-erage is partial. The register-gradient matrix (Table 10, Section 5.5) extends the paired E14 measurement from the 8B zero-shot floor to both aligned checkpoints across the template and LLM-rewritten registers; multi-turn, real-user phrasing remains unmeasured. 4. The conversion repair is not end-to-end on real services. E16/E17 use oracle/annotation-grounded returns; Eval A’s fallback rate is 1.0. End-to-end success against live expert backends is not yet measured, and the routing give-back (tool selection 0.914 → 0.868 ; calls 384 → 433 ) is the price of the repair. 5. RWA2 ’s routing-first shape carries a call-versus-answer tension. Calling correctness and answer correctness diverge in the data (Table 4: ArgV 0.951 /0.963 vs. Summ 0.020 /0.025 for E13/E15), and E17’s conversion repair buys answer accuracy at a routing give-back (tool selection 

0.914 → 0.868 , intrinsic routing 0.985 → 0.913 ; Section 5.7) — a two-dimensional trade-off we disclose rather than resolve. 6. Unseen-tool generalization is negative: registration makes a tool visible, not usable. 

All three trained checkpoints route 0/62 on the training-unseen sm3det records, with emissions collapsing onto the seen tools (48 / 3 / 11; Section 4.3). The test pool is three tools; routing generalization under a larger catalog is untested. 7. The region parameter p of T_call (ek, p ) is not evaluated. In the reported runs the tools consume the full image or the query text as the prompt; region grounding, the syntax of p, and a 30 region-correctness metric are unmeasured, although acting on the right image region is the core UHR difficulty (a target may cover 0.14% of the scene; Figure 1). 8. The tool pool is generic CV, not GIS. Detection, segmentation and counting backends only; no GIS operations (region statistics, overlay, buffering, area/distance measurement) and no use of CRS, GSD or georegistration — a geospatial workflow case is future work. 9. Intrinsic tasks are likely too easy. The zero-shot model answers essentially all intrinsic records (600/600), so intrinsic routing accuracy carries little signal; a harder intrinsic split is needed. 10. Training queries are templated. Template-mode vagueness is narrower than open-ended user phrasing ( vague_level all 0.5); the LLM rewrite channel exists but is not enabled for training. 11. Prompt fragility is a deployment risk. The E12 audit shows that one prompt-side edit can silently destroy trained routing: the same weights emit on 0.65 of tool tasks under the training-format prompt but 0/400 under the evaluation-format prompt (Section 6.1). 12. Change detection collapses to detection. Absent bi-temporal sources, the change-detection shape is collapsed to detection (Section 4.2), so temporal reasoning is outside the evaluated scope. 13. Benchmark contamination and geographic concentration. Test records derive from public benchmarks (LRS-VQA, MME-RealWorld-RS, XLRS-Bench), so pretraining exposure of the base VLM cannot be excluded, and the sources’ geographic coverage is limited (the flagged 1213 cluster; Section 4.4). 14. Stratified estimates, not full benchmarks. Except for the 300-record official comparison, all benchmark numbers are stratified estimates; sampling noise is quantified only through paired tests, and all runs use a single seed (seed 0). XLRS: 7 of 8 subcategories (SR unavailable; OP 53.9% of our sample). 15. Mixed tool backends. Tool experiments mix real deployments (DirectSAM-class) with annotation-grounded simulated backends, disclosed per table; a deployment attempt of the SkySense/ RemoteSAM service stack on the evaluation box is recorded (mmdet/mmcv and service weights absent; Section 5.7), and live end-to-end evaluation remains future work. 16. One retained annotation misread. Of the six audited records from the flagged 1213 cluster, five were confirmed and one source-annotation misread is retained unchanged to keep the released test set byte-identical; the correction is scheduled for the next corpus revision (Section 4.4). 

# 8 Conclusion 

WeaveAgent is a two-stage tool-routing agent for ultra-high-resolution remote sensing. Stage A is a routing-first decision stage: the ±-image ablation (E19) shows its emission suppression is visually grounded — removing image tokens keeps extrinsic routing but collapses intrinsic routing to 0.022 — and it performs intrinsic/extrinsic discrimination and tool selection from the query and the cat-alog, on the design basis established by the text-routing literature. Stage B executes conditionally: the intrinsic path answers from the thumbnail with an optional fixed-budget evidence-compression interface; the extrinsic path executes T_call on the original full-resolution imagery and answers from tool observations along two-turn trajectories with observation masking. On the VagueUHR dual-split corpus, the alignment-SFT → RWA2 GRPO recipe yields its checkable gain at the align-ment stage: alignment SFT lifts extrinsic routing from 0% (zero-shot: zero emissions) to 80.75% (323/400) with tool selection 0.905; the RWA2 GRPO stage leaves extrinsic tool selection unchanged 31 (323/400), suppresses 9 intrinsic mis-emissions (calls 398 → 384 , intrinsic routing 0.970 → 0.985 ;answer accuracy classification 0.435 → 0.460 , reasoning 0.435 → 0.450 ), and moves overall accuracy 

0.2430 → 0.2500 (within the noise band) — with healthy training dynamics (zero-variance groups an order of magnitude below the unaligned arms). The evidence board is honestly positioned as a fixed-budget compression interface: two views of ≈5k tokens at constant cost regardless of source size, with a 1.31 × detail ceiling and three-benchmark paired deltas of up to −3.4 points reported as such; single-pass evidence construction runs at 7.31 s per sample on a consumer 4090 — on par with re-ported A100 numbers and faster than reported multi-round visual search (cross-hardware reference). Where the pipeline broke, we said so and repaired what could be repaired: an oracle study attributed the extrinsic answer gap to observation →answer conversion (argument values ≈96% correct, final answers 0.045), and extrinsic answer accuracy rose from 0.025 to 0.425 by loading the observation alone and 0.518 with the two-turn SFT stage ( p = 3 .8 × 10 −5), under marker-free cross-mode re-turns (0.965 under training-distribution returns), at a small routing cost. A query-register gradient measurement (Section 5.5) closes the wording question for the aligned checkpoints: template-level vagueness moves overall accuracy by at most 1.2 points, while LLM-rewritten phrasing costs 3.1

(E15) and 10 .9 (E17) points overall with a ≈14 -point extrinsic-routing give-back — measured, not assumed. Open problems are equally explicit: 8B-scale alignment, end-to-end conversion repair against live tool services, register coverage beyond the two measured registers (multi-turn, real-user phrasing), and a truly text-only router — E19 shows emission suppression is visually grounded, so distilling one is future work. Code, data, and all evaluation protocols will be released. 

Data and code availability. The VagueUHR corpus is derived from the imagery and annota-tions of LRS-VQA, MME-RealWorld-RS and XLRS-Bench and is distributed under the terms of those benchmarks’ licenses. Construction scripts, prompt templates, per-experiment configurations, reward code, and all evaluation protocols will be released at a public repository upon acceptance.
