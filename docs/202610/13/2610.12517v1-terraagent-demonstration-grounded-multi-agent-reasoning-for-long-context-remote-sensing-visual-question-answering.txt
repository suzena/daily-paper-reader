# TerraAgent: Demonstration-Grounded Multi-Agent Reason-ing for Long-Context Remote Sensing Visual Question An-swering 

Jingwen Huang, Yiming Fang, Hanxiao Wu, Boyuan He, Keyi Luo 

Yunnan University of Finance and Economics, Kunming, China 

keyi.luo@kust.edu.cn 

## Abstract 

Remote sensing visual question answering (RS-VQA) requires answers that truly depend on image evidence, yet current remote sensing multimodal large language models (RS MLLMs) are still evaluated mainly by accuracy, which cannot reveal whether a correct answer was produced without us-ing the image. This setting introduces three challenges: demonstrations retrieved across different ground sample distances (GSD), sensors, and city textures may be poorly matched; real queries often involve long visual contexts from multi-scale tiles, multi-temporal frames, and retrieved knowl-edge, where evidence can be diluted and errors can propagate through multi-step reasoning; and prior-only answering remains invisible to stan-dard metrics. We present AVA , a training-free framework that wraps a frozen RS MLLM with three modules. GDR performs GSD-bucketed, scene-graph-based demonstration retrieval and filters demonstrations whose answers are unsupported by their images. MACE decomposes each query into an evidence-typed sub-question DAG, routes sub-questions to suitable perception tools, and records verified provenance in a shared evidence ledger with bounded re-perception. CV A audits the final answer through counterfactual perturbations of cited evidence, including ablation and swap, and computes a visual-dependency score for confidence calibra-tion and evidence-forcing re-query. On RSVQA-LR/HR, VRSBench, and LHRS-Bench, AVA improves average accuracy by up to 2.40 points over the strongest same-setting baselines, raises the visual-dependency score from 0.28 to 0.49, reduces the prior-only answer rate from 46.2% to 21.7%, and lowers the hallucination rate from 17.1% to 9.8%, with 2.6 × the cost of a single backbone forward pass and no fine-tuning. 

## 1 Introduction 

Remote sensing visual question answering (RS-VQA) is the interface through which earth-observation systems turn pixels into decisions: damage assessment, illegal-construction screening and urban-expansion monitoring all end with a natural-language judgement about what is there , what changed and what it means Lobry et al. (2020). In the last two years, remote sensing multimodal large language models (RS MLLMs) such as GeoChat Kuckreja et al. (2023), LHRS-Bot Muhtar et al. (2024), RSGPT Hu et al. (2023) and SkyEyeGPT Zhan et al. (2024) have pushed zero-shot RS-VQA accuracy to levels that were unreachable with task-specific pipelines, mostly by scaling instruction data and domain adaptation. Yet three properties of the remote sensing setting remain unresolved, and they are precisely the ones that determine whether such a model can be deployed. 

The central question. A single number hides the whole story. On RSVQA-LR, a model that never sees the image and answers from the question text alone already reaches 80.90% overall accuracy, whereas the full image–text model reaches 87.14% Wang & Ghamisi (2024). Most of the reported accuracy is therefore recoverable from language priors: “is there a building?” has a skewed answer distribution, and “how many” questions collapse to a 1arXiv:2610.12517v1 [cs.CV] 7 Oct 2026 handful of values. Accuracy cannot tell us whether an answer came from the image or from the question. We therefore treat visual dependency — the causal sensitivity of an answer to the visual evidence it cites — as a first-class quantity, not an after-the-fact analysis. 

Why demonstrations do not transfer. In-context learning is the cheapest way to adapt a large vision–language model to a new task Zhou et al. (2024), and retrieval-augmented variants have been ported to remote sensing, either over knowledge Wen et al. (2026) or over imagery at scale Rajendran et al. (2026); Kazoom et al. (2026). However, RS imagery spans three orders of magnitude of ground sample distance (GSD): a Sentinel-2 tile at 10 m and a USGS orthophoto at 15 cm show the same “urban density” question through entirely different texture and object sizes. Cosine similarity over global image embeddings confuses a low-resolution city with a high-resolution field, and once several retrieved demonstrations share an answer (six “yes” out of eight), the model copies that distribution instead of reading the query image. What is missing is a comparability measure that is scale-normalised and a reject rule that removes demonstrations whose answer is not grounded in their own image. 

Why long contexts hurt. Real queries are answered over long visual contexts: a large scene plus multi-scale crops (a small object occupies a few dozen pixels at full extent), multi-temporal frames, and external knowledge. Once the context grows, large vision– language models systematically shift weight from visual evidence to textual cues, and 2026 evaluations of agentic systems report three recurring failure modes — domain-toolset mismatch, unchecked error propagation, and static reasoning Zhang et al. (2026); Xiao et al. (2026). A five-step chain with 5% per-step error can accumulate to roughly 23% end-to-end error, and no current RS agent makes “every claim must have provenance” a hard constraint, although planner training for long-horizon agent tasks is known to dominate local heuristics Si et al. (2026). 

Contributions. We present TerraAgent (Figure 1), a training-free framework that equips a frozen RS MLLM with demonstration grounding, evidence-carrying collaboration, and counterfactual auditing. Our contributions are: • GDR (Geo-Discriminative Retrieval). We replace single-vector retrieval with GSD-bucketed, dual-granularity matching (global RemoteCLIP embedding plus a structured scene graph over class counts, a 3 × 3 position histogram and a scale bucket), followed by an evidence-consistency gate (ECG) which asks the frozen backbone to re-answer each candidate demonstration from its image alone and discards candidates whose stored answer is not reproduced. The gate removes 12% of the demonstration pool. • MACE (Multi-Agent Collaborative Evidence). We instantiate role-specialised collaboration in an evidence-centric form: a planner emits an evidence-typed sub-question DAG, a perceiver routes each sub-question to the task-appropriate tool, every intermediate result is written as a typed entry with provenance and confidence into a shared evidence ledger, and a verifier rejects unsupported spans and triggers bounded re-perception. The ledger turns error propagation from a statistical risk into a checkable invariant. • CVA (Counterfactual Visual-dependency Audit). We make visual dependency measurable at inference time. For each surviving answer we run two perturbations of the evidence it cites — precise ablation of the cited region (or key temporal frame) and replacement with same-class evidence from another location — and define the visual-dependency score (VDS) and evidence-swap sensitivity (ESS). Low-VDS answers are re-queried with evidence-forcing prompts or abstained, and VDS is used to calibrate confidence. • Results and diagnosis. On RSVQA-LR/HR Lobry et al. (2020), VRSBench Li et al. (2024) and LHRS-Bench Muhtar et al. (2024), TerraAgent improves the strongest same-setting baseline by +1.59, +1.55, +2.40 and +1.74 points respectively, while raising VDS from 0.28 to 0.49, lowering the prior-only answer rate from 46.2% to 21.7% and the judged hallucination rate from 17.1% to 9.8%. We further show that longer demonstration contexts help and then hurt (§5.3), that the gain grows with 2Figure 1: Overview of TerraAgent. ## 2 Related Work 

Remote sensing visual question answering. RSVQA established the task and the low-/high-resolution splits that are still standard Lobry et al. (2020); VRSBench broadened evaluation to nine question categories with human-verified captions and object references Li et al. (2024); LHRS-Bench provides an objective single-choice probe of eleven remote sensing capability dimensions with global 1 m imagery Muhtar et al. (2024). Parameter-efficient adaptation of pretrained multimodal transformers remains competitive there Wang & Ghamisi (2024). Our work keeps these benchmarks but changes what is reported: alongside accuracy we report how much of the accuracy depends on the image. 

Remote sensing and multimodal agents. Two 2026 systems are closest to us. GeoMMA-gent orchestrates retrieval, perception and reasoning specialists over a geoscience benchmark and shows that tool-augmented agents beat standalone models Xiao et al. (2026); UAV-MAS is a training-free multi-agent system whose ablation identifies domain-toolset mismatch, unchecked error propagation and static reasoning as the dominant failure modes Zhang et al. (2026). Role-specialised collaboration was shown to outperform monolithic models earlier in the multimodal medical setting Zhou et al. (2025). Our MACE reuses the planning skeleton but makes the intermediate product a ledger with provenance, and our verifier is a rejection rule over that ledger rather than another voting agent. 

Retrieval augmentation for multimodal questions. Retrieving outside knowledge for VQA Lin & Byrne (2022), multimodal retrieval-augmented generation over images and text Chen et al. (2022), hypergraph reasoning for knowledge-based VQA Heo et al. (2022) and retrieval-augmented captioning Ramos et al. (2023) all assume that “more relevant context” equals “better answer”. Remote sensing retrieval has followed suit, from damage-assessment pipelines at scale Rajendran et al. (2026) to knowledge-conditioned RS-RAG Wen et al. (2026) and retrieval-curated change benchmarks Kazoom et al. (2026). We instead show that in remote sensing the selection of demonstrations is a scale-matching problem, and that a consistency gate is necessary to prevent answer-distribution leakage. 

Demonstration-based in-context learning and sample selection. Visual in-context learn-ing demonstrated that a handful of image–answer demonstrations can substitute for task-3specific training Zhou et al. (2024), and long-context alignment work has shown that select-ing influential samples matters more than using more samples Si et al. (2025). Dual-encoder distillation Wang et al. (2022) supplies the retrieval encoder family we build on. Sample selection in remote sensing additionally has to respect GSD and sensor identity, which no text- or embedding-similarity criterion captures. 

Long context, planning and faithfulness. Text-side long-context QA benefits from super-vised contrastive objectives Caciularu et al. (2022); planning with world models Hao et al. (2023) and prompting surveys Qiao et al. (2023) provide the decomposition vocabulary of our planner, and training global planners for long-horizon agent tasks has been shown to dominate local heuristics Si et al. (2026). Faithfulness under long contexts can be improved by synthetic supervision and reinforcement learning Si et al. (2026), but those methods train the language model; TerraAgent instead constrains inference with provenance and coun-terfactual evidence perturbation. The visual-dependency phenomenon — models drifting towards textual priors as context grows — was documented for long-context reasoning Zhou et al. (2024); we turn it into a measurable, actionable signal. 

Remote sensing change understanding and long-context evaluation. Recent work names the underlying deficiency “temporal blindness” and proposes change-enhanced attention for multi-temporal interpretation Li et al. (2026); SMART-HC-VQA formalises sparse, irregular multi-temporal observations as a long-context VQA protocol Ramirez et al. (2026); AVION adapts vision–language models to aerial imagery with prompt tuning Hu et al. (2026). Our evaluation protocol borrows the spirit of these works — multi-image contexts and explicit perturbation — but keeps the benchmarks standard so that results remain comparable.

## 4 Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate on four dataset configurations that stress different axes. RSVQA-LR (772 Sentinel-2 images, 10 m GSD, four question types) and RSVQA-HR (10,659 aerial images at 15 cm, four question types, with a cross-city test split) test scale sensitivity Lobry et al. (2020). VRSBench tests fine-grained question types: nine categories over 29,614 images and 123,221 question–answer pairs drawn from DOTA-v2 and DIOR Li et al. (2024). LHRS-Bench tests objective domain knowledge and perception through 690 human-annotated single-choice questions over eleven dimensions on global 1 m imagery Muhtar et al. (2024). 

Protocol. Following prior work we report the average accuracy over question types, omitting counting questions on RSVQA-LR and counting plus area questions on RSVQA-HR; VRSBench is evaluated with the official GPT-based open-set judge, and LHRS-Bench with exact single-choice match. TerraAgent is evaluated without any backbone fine-tuning and without training on benchmark question–answer pairs: the demonstration pool is disjoint from the evaluation splits. 

Backbone and implementation. Unless stated otherwise the backbone is GeoChat-7B Kuckreja et al. (2023), an RS instruction-tuned LLaVA-1.5 model Liu et al. (2023; 2024); for LHRS-Bench we additionally report LHRS-Bot-7B as backbone to show that the framework is backbone-agnostic. The retrieval index is built on RemoteCLIP Liu et al. (2024); the perception tools use an open-vocabulary detector, a bi-temporal differencing module and an RS scene classifier. Default hyper-parameters are K = 6 demonstrations, λ = 0.5, τs

set so that 11.4% of spans need re-perception, r = 2 and τvd = 0.35. The 2026 change-understanding and long-context protocols Li et al. (2026); Ramirez et al. (2026) inform our multi-image evaluation, but we keep the benchmark suites standard. 

Metrics. Beyond accuracy we report VDS and ESS (Equation 15), the prior-only answer rate (the fraction of samples answered correctly even after the cited evidence is ablated), the judged hallucination rate and the unsupported-span ratio, latency in seconds, input tokens, tool calls and relative local inference cost.

The quantity column deserves a comment: the spread is extreme (26.0 for MiniGPT-v2 versus 93.6 for TerraAgent) because counting is exactly the question type where a captioning model fails and a de-duplicated counting tool succeeds, which is the empirical justification for MACE’s routing table. Table 3 reports LHRS-Bench, the least gameable of the three suites because each item is a single-choice question authored by remote sensing experts. This is a meaningful negative result rather than noise. Resolution asks what sensor or resolution this image comes from , and distance asks for metric judgements; both are better supported by the vast sensor-geography knowledge accumulated during pretraining of closed models than by any perception tool invoked on the current image. Our framework improves perceptual grounding , not world knowledge, and the two must be improved by different means.

Figure 2(b) sweeps the demonstration budget. Accuracy rises from 76.0 at K = 0 to 80.2 at K = 6 and then falls back to 79.2 at K = 12. 5.5 Does the model actually look at the image? 

Figure 3 and Table 7 answer the question that motivated the paper. When we ablate the evidence the model itself cited, LLaVA-1.5 loses only 0.11 of its accuracy, GeoChat 0.28 and LHRS-Bot 0.20: their correct answers are only weakly caused by the image. TerraAgent loses 46.2 points on the same probe (VDS 0.49), and replacing the cited evidence with same-class evidence from elsewhere changes its answer 34% of the time (ESS 0.34), against 3% for GeoChat and 2% for LHRS-Bot. In other words, the single-pass models frequently produce the right token for the wrong reason, and accuracy cannot see it. The consequences are visible in downstream faithfulness: TerraAgent’s judged hallucination rate is 9.8% against 16.4–21.3% for the baselines, and its unsupported-span ratio is 11.4%, i.e. roughly nine out of ten claims are traceable to a ledger entry. We stress the direction of these numbers: for the ablation and swap rows, lower accuracy is better , because it means the answer collapses when the evidence does. 

5.6 Efficiency and failure modes 

Table 8 and Figure 4 summarise the trade-off. TerraAgent costs 2.6 local forward passes per query (4.6 s, 3.4k input tokens, 2.3 tool calls), which is 2.4 × a single pass but still 1.8 × faster than a closed-model API call, and it spends no fine-tuning budget. The decomposition of Equation 18 is informative: retrieval costs one pass, the MACE loop averages 1.3 passes because only 11.4% of spans trigger re-perception, and the audit costs two passes on a shortened context. Because the audit perturbs only the cited entries, its cost does not grow with K.

Failure modes. We inspected the residual errors and group them into four categories. (i) 

Tool-limited counting : dense clusters of small objects (parked cars, boats) still under-count because the detector merges instances; the verifier can flag the disagreement but cannot recover the count. (ii) Knowledge-bound items : resolution and distance questions (Table 3) 11 (a) Capability profile on LHRS-Bench. (b) Accuracy versus latency.

Bucket misassignment : when the GSD regressor puts a 0.3 m image into the 1 m bucket, retrieval drifts to visually plausible but metrically different demonstrations; this is rare but it produces confident errors. (iv) Ambiguous ground truth : for area questions with a hard threshold on building counts, the benchmark annotation itself is debatable, and the verifier’s demand for provenance then leads to abstention where a human would also hesitate. We consider (ii) and (iv) to be properties of the benchmarks rather than of the framework, whereas (i) and (iii) are clear targets for future work. 

## 6 Conclusion 

TerraAgent argues that remote sensing question answering should be evaluated not only by whether the answer is right but by whether the answer would change if the image changed. The framework combines scale-aware, evidence-gated demonstration retrieval with role-specialised agents that must carry provenance, and with a counterfactual audit that turns visual dependency into a measurable signal usable at inference time. Across four benchmark configurations the resulting system improves accuracy by 1.55 to 2.40 points over the strongest same-setting baselines, more than doubles the visual-dependency score, halves the prior-only answer rate and nearly halves the hallucination rate, at 2.6 × the cost of a single forward pass and without any fine-tuning. The same experiments also delimit what the approach cannot do: knowledge-bound dimensions such as sensor identification and metric distance remain the province of large pretrained models, and dense small-object counting remains tool-limited. 

Limitations and future work. Our audit assumes that the model can name the evidence it uses; if a correct answer has no citable entry, the pipeline abstains even when the answer is right, which costs accuracy on high-prior questions. The GSD regressor is trained on a coarse three-way bucketisation and is the single most likely component to misroute retrieval. The current study is also limited to optical imagery; synthetic aperture radar and LiDAR would require additional perception tools, and temporal reasoning is only exercised through pairwise frames rather than through learned change operators. Finally, we report VDS against perturbations that we design; adversarial perturbations that preserve semantics while changing ground truth would give a stronger, if harder, test. 12 Acknowledgements
