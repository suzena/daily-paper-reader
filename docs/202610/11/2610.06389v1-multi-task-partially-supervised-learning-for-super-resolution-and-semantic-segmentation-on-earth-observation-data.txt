ABSTRACT 

Super-resolution and semantic segmentation are known to benefit one another, especially in the Earth observation context. However, learning both tasks in a joint model often requires both task annotations, which is impractical and expensive. In this paper, we study the multi-task par-tially supervised learning paradigm for both tasks, where each example is assumed to have only a single-task an-notation. To that end, we examine two multi-task archi-tectural variations, the sequential and shared variants, then propose a hybrid variant and a re-projection loss to benefit from the shared representation and enforce image quality of super-resolution when training with semantic segmenta-tion. Experiments show favorable results compared to the SOTAs sequential variant. Source codes will be published at 

https://github.com/lhoangan/munera .

Index Terms — super-resolution, semantic segmentation, multi-task, partially supervised learning 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Earth observation, which studies and monitors the character-istics and activities on the Earth’s surface, has been attracting the attention of the computer vision community because of its importance and potential. One prominent challenge of Earth observation data is the high variation of data appearances due to geographical differences, e.g . rural compared to urban ar-eas, and due to different capturing sensors and acquisition al-titudes, e.g . aerial and satellite platforms. Scaling variation also affects scenes’ detail level, which is generally crucial for semantic-based applications [1, 2]. Super-resolution, which upsamples images and recovers high-resolution details, could serve as a remedy. However, it is an ill-posed problem, as there exists, in principle, an infi-nite number of high-resolution images that, through different degrading processes, result in the same low-resolution input image [3]. Categorical information has proven effective in constraining super-resolution [4], allowing realistic textural reconstruction [5, 6] and improving photo-realistic qualities.

As it is known that the multi-task paradigm learns domain-specific information from related tasks to improve one an-other, jointly learning super-resolution and semantic segmen-tation in a single model should be reciprocally beneficial for Earth observation applications. The dual-task model by Wang et al . [7] with a shared encoder and two task-heads enhancing representation detail levels and predicting seman-tic segmentation at larger resolutions has been applied to remote sensing by various authors [8, 9] while being adapted to different network architectures [10]. On the other hand, [6] and [4] jointly learn semantic segmentation and super-resolution for the super-resolution’s benefit. Although var-ious approaches in the literature have leveraged one task to improve the performance of the other [5, 6, 7, 8], focusing on both tasks simultaneously [9, 10] remains limited, due to the impractical requirements of annotations for all tasks per training example that multiply the effort in creating a dataset and maintaining task synchronization. Multi-task partially supervised learning [11], in which each training example is required to be annotated for only a single task, is proposed to relax the strict requirement of multi-task learning. Without requiring all-task annotations for each training example, multiple datasets already anno-tated for different tasks can be combined in a single training pipeline, increasing the number of training examples and benefiting from complementary information. Yet, it is not without hindrances. The lack of joint annotations hinders joint optimization during training and requires specific atten-tion regarding the tasks and input data [12]. In this paper, we jointly learn super-resolution and semantic segmentation under the same network with a shared encoder using partially annotated datasets for Earth observation. To that end, we propose a re-projection loss to constrain the super-resolution subnet when training with segmentation-annotated data. Despite several works on discriminative tasks such as depth, segmentation, and object detection, the combination of a generative (super-resolution) and a discriminative (se-mantic segmentation) task is still limited, especially for Earth observation. Similar to our setting are the semantic-guided super-resolution [4] and super-resolution for image recog-nition (SR4IR) [2], which places a semantic segmentation network after the super-resolution network in a sequential or-

Although such a design orients super-resolution toward a downstream task, it places semantic segmentation under the entire influence of super-resolution performance and reduces shared representation. To make the best of both worlds, we propose a hybrid variant that allows shared features as well as adapting super-resolution for a downstream task. The experiments performed on the ISPRS [13] and proprietary Pleiades-based datasets show favorable results compared to the existing state-of-the-art. The contributions of the paper are as follows: We (1) study the multi-task partial supervised learning for super-resolution and semantic segmentation on Earth observation data, and (2) propose a hybrid variant and a re-projection loss to enforce super-resolution image quality.

2.2. Network architecture 

The network overview in Figure 1(a) visualizes with color-code a shared encoder E(·) for feature extraction and two task-decoder heads, DcR(·) and DS (·) for super-resolution and semantic segmentation, respectively. The predictions are, 

˜Hx = DcR (E(Lx)) and ˜Mx = DS (E(Lx)) . (3) For super-resolution, we employ the RRDBNet architec-ture utilized in the ESRGAN [14] model with 23 residual-in-residual dense blocks (RRDB) to retain high-level informa-tion and increase the encoder’s capacity before upsampling 4 times (via interpolations and convolutions) and produc-ing the super-resolved image. For semantic segmentation, the HRNet [15] architecture is employed, which maintains a stream of embeddings at the input size. As the RRDBNet encodes the input image while maintaining the resolution of the embeddings, it replaces the first stage of HRNet and acts as the shared encoder E, leaving the upsampling subnet the super-resolution decoder DcR, with 2 pairs of interpolation-convolution layers (for c = 4 ) followed by 2 convolution layers. The rest of HRNet becomes the semantic segmenta-tion head DS . Interested readers are referred to the original works for more details. As we do not make any assumption on the architecture, this model is architecture-agnostic. 

Multi-task decoders Different from the shared -encoder approach in conventional multi-task models, [2, 4] proposed a sequential approach which concatenates a full HRNet to the RRDBNet such that the super-resolved images are fed directly to the HRNet and uses the segmentation loss to guide the super-resolution performance (Figure 1-(b)). This approach allows maintaining the gradient flow through the super-resolution head when training with segmentation-annotated inputs LS , thereby putting the segmentation per-formance on the entire dependency of super-resolution. To make the best of both worlds, we propose a hybrid head (Fig-ure 1-(c)) where the shared features are adapted and added to the super-resolved features. The three variants share the same encoder E(.) and two decoders DR(.) and DS (.), as indicated by the same color code. The added blocks to adapt the features are shown explicitly for clarity purposes. 

2.3. Optimizing objectives 

The loss function for super-resolution and semantic segmen-tation task, respectively, can be written as LSR (f (LR) ˜H , H R)

and LSS (f (LS ) ˜M , M S ), which receive different input vari-ables as partial multi-task learning assumes a single-task an-notation per training image. The optimization and parame-ters update can be performed collectively by alternating im-age batches for each task as inputs, making two batches per iteration, or separately for each task every epoch. 

Super-resolution task Two losses are employed, in-cluding the reconstruction or pixel loss Lpix that measures the similarity to the high-resolution target using the L1 dis-tance and the Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) [16] loss Lpcp with AlexNet. We deliberately with-hold the adversarial loss, as the ablation study in Table 3 shows that it destabilizes the training process and produces suboptimal performance. The final loss is as follows: 

LSR = λ1Lpix ( ˜H, H ) + λ2Lpcp ( ˜H, H ), (4) 

Semantic segmentation task For the semantic segmen-tation head, the cross-entropy with softmax loss LCE is em-ployed. To further constrain the super-resolution head, the super-resolved image is re-projected to the low-resolution do-main using nearest neighbor, and a re-projection loss is ap-plied with the input image to constrain the fidelity of the out-put, i.e . Lrpj ( ˜HS ↓c, L S ) = L1( ˜HS ↓c, L S ). The total loss for data annotated with semantic segmentation is, therefore: 

LSS = LCE ( ˜MS , M S ) + λ3Lrpj ( ˜HS ↓c, L S ). (5) 

λ1, λ2, and λ3 are empirically set to 0.01, 1, 4, respectively.

3.2. Partial multi-task learning 

In this subsection, the proposed variants for partial multi-task learning (PMTL) are compared to the state-of-the-art ap-proaches using the sequential variant, namely SR4IR [2] and SS-guided SR [4]. To maintain comparability, we follow each paper’s description and re-implement using our codebase. In particular, we respect the choices for losses and their em-pirical weights, as well as initialize all networks from pre-trained models. For SR4IR [2], the CQMix augmentation is removed for semantic segmentation as it violates the assump-tion of multi-task partially supervised learning. All networks are trained for 200K iterations with early stopping at the high-est mIOU. 

For super-resolution the quantitative results on IS-PRS test set are shown in Table 1. The PMTL approaches outperform both state-of-the-art methods on structure simi-larity (SSIM) and achieve lower distortion (PSNR), showing higher image quality. 4. CONCLUSION 

The paper studies the joint learning under the multi-task par-tially supervised framework for Earth observation imagery. It is based on the well-known assumption that the two tasks are highly relevant and could contribute to one another. The hy-brid architecture is proposed to inherit the best of both shared representation and the semantic-guided sequential variants. The experiments against SOTA sequential methods show higher super-resolution quality both quantitatively and visu-ally, as well as similar semantic segmentation performance with a slight numerical benefit. 55.
