# Reinforcement Learning-Based 3D Beam Adaptation for Underwater Wireless Optical Communication with AUVs 

Viviana Centritto Arrojo, Ama Bandara, Sergi Abadal, Evgenii Vinogradov 

Nanonetworking Center in Catalunya, Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya, Barcelona, Spain 

Abstract —Underwater Wireless Optical Communications (UWOC) provide essential high data rates for Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs), but reliable connectivity is criti-cally affected by transmitter-receiver misalignment. This work addresses the beam pointing problem for a moving AUV subject to unknown ocean currents through a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework. We develop a comprehensive 3D UWOC channel model incorporating depth-dependent atten-uation, turbulence, and a discrete-ray method to accurately quantify geometric and misalignment losses. The resulting agent jointly optimizes beam steering and divergence angles, learning a policy that prioritizes continuous link maintenance. Evaluated using real oceanographic data, the framework’s performance is assessed via the excess outage metric, which isolates outages occurring exclusively due to pointing errors. Relative to perfect alignment between nodes, the proposed method bounds this metric to 8.5% under ocean current-free tracking conditions and limits it to 16.2% when subjected to current-induced drift. The findings obtained in this work confirms that DRL-enabled beam control is capable of adaptive tracking and mitigating link outages in dynamic underwater environments. 

Index Terms —underwater wireless optical communications, 3D beam adaptation, deep reinforcement learning. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

UWOC has emerged as an essential technology for high-capacity underwater links, overcoming the severe attenuation of radio frequency (RF) signals and the bandwidth limitations of acoustic systems [1]. Characterized by high data rates and low latency, UWOC is a relevant solution for integrating underwater networks into beyond 5G and 6G ecosystems [2]. Nevertheless, the underwater environment presents a chan-nel plagued by impairments, leading to severe problems of pointing accuracy [3].While environmental factors degrade signal quality, a lack of alignment between the transmitter and receiver can produce a complete link outage. This alignment is further compromised by ocean currents, which introduce drift and jitter, resulting in significant positional uncertainty [2], [4]. In this context, there is a need for autonomous systems capable of processing high amounts of heterogeneous information to make optimal real-time decisions. The objective of such systems is to ensure the reliability of the communication link in harsh dynamic environments. Recent literature has demonstrated the efficacy of reinforce-ment learning (RL) algorithms in addressing UWOC pointing challenges. Romdhane and Kaddoum [5] proposed a dynamic RL beam width orientation and adaptation framework. This was achieved by implementing model-free Q-learning and State-Action-Reward-State-Action (SARSA) algorithms to op-timize link performance for underwater nodes. To improve upon reactive approaches, the work in [6] integrates Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) with Deep-Q Networks (DQNs), leveraging the LSTM to predict vessel displacement based on historical wave data, and enabling the DQN agent to optimize both the beam divergence and steering angles. Furthermore, to maintain a seabed-to-vessel link disrupted by waves, the au-thors in [7] propose a two-agent DRL framework. The system divides the tracking tasks temporally: one agent updates the beam’s azimuth and elevation over longer intervals, while a second agent preemptively fine-tunes the divergence at every time slot to ensure coverage. However, prior studies largely rely on 2D spatial models or focus on surface-bound receivers disrupted by surface waves. By treating the underwater channel as a homogeneous environment, they cannot model depth-dependent variations in attenuation, turbulence and noise. Consequently, 3D tracking for submerged, mobile AUVs remains unresolved. We propose a dynamic 3D DRL framework that incorporates vertical link characteristics and underwater current-induced positional un-certainty. To accurately quantify geometric and pointing losses, our approach evaluates the individual optical rays failing to reach the receiver aperture. Therefore, the main contributions of this work are: (i) char-acterization of the 3D UWOC environment; and (ii) design and evaluation of a RL-based DQN control framework that maintains beam alignment and link quality. At the start of every episode, a unique R x trajectory is generated via an interpolated 3D random walk, bounded between 1 and 50 m in depth with simulated AUV velocities ranging from 0.6 to 1.2 m/s. Moving beyond individual test cases, the agent’s overall performance is assessed across 1,500 unseen episodes by measuring the percentage of simulation time experiencing communication outages ( SN R < 10 dB). To isolate outages caused by depth-dependent channel effects, which are be-yond the agent’s control, we compare the agent’s per-episode outage duration against a baseline that assumes perfect R x-AUV tracking. The difference between both is the excess outage metric, which quantifies the fraction of simulation time where connectivity is lost exclusively due to pointing errors. As depicted in Fig. 8, the histograms for both scenarios are positively skewed with the primary peak located at the lower bound, corresponding to episodes with zero tracking-induced signal loss. Under current-free conditions, the mean excess outage is 8.5%, indicating that, on average, pointing errors account for 0.8 s of signal loss during the total 10-second simulation. However, in current-induced trajectories, this metric increases to 16.2%. This performance degradation reflects the increased complexity of the task, as the agent must simultaneously manage pointing control, estimate current-induced drift, and predict the R x-AUV’s actual position. VI. C ONCLUSION 

This work presented a 3D DRL framework to address transmitter-receiver misalignment between mobile AUVs. To model this environment, we developed a 3D UWOC channel incorporating depth-dependent impairments, and a discrete-ray mechanism to simulate pointing loss. Evaluating 1,500 trajec-tories, we assessed the framework using the excess outage metric, which strictly accounts for the pointing accuracy of the agent. Under positional certainty, the agent successfully tracked the R x, limiting the mean excess outage to 8.5% when compared to ideal conditions without pointing errors. In the scenario where the R x’s trajectory is perturbed by ocean currents, the system constrained this metric to 16.2%. These results demonstrate that the agent learned a baseline policy to estimate oceanic drift and preserve link reliability by simultaneously controlling beam direction and divergence. - - Rx aperture Fig. 7.
